Orbital Debris & Space Clutter

Understanding the Growing Threat to Earth Orbit

Research Overview

Orbital debris represents one of the most pressing challenges facing modern spacefaring nations and commercial space operations. As humanity continues to expand its presence in Earth orbit, the accumulation of defunct satellites, spent rocket stages, and fragmentation debris has created an increasingly hazardous environment.

This research project examines the distribution, characterization, and impact of orbital debris across multiple altitude regimes. Through comprehensive analysis of historical and contemporary satellite catalogs, we investigate the dynamics of space clutter and its implications for current and future orbital missions.

The Kessler Syndrome—a theoretical cascade of collisions that could render certain orbits unusable for generations—makes this research not merely academic, but critical to the sustainable use of space. Our findings contribute to a deeper understanding of debris mitigation strategies and collision avoidance protocols.
