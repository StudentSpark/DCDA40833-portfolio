Introduction

Orbital debris is a cumulative artifact of the space age: dead satellites, spent launch stages, mission fragments, and untracked clutter that persists long after the original mission ends. At orbital speeds, even tiny objects carry enough energy to threaten active systems.

The result is an environment that behaves less like empty space and more like contested infrastructure. This research treats debris as a measurable operational constraint, asking how it evolves, where it concentrates, and what its trajectory implies for the future of space access.

Near-future operations depend on clean situational awareness. Understanding clutter is part of keeping orbit usable.
